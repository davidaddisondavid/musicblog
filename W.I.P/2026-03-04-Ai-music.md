&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I tend to have a pretty strong kneejerk reaction against anything ai generated. I really hate the condescending, overwrought voice of Chat GPT which has become the dominant language of the internet, I hate the endless slurry of lazy corporate ai adverts & the cacophony of cloying ai memes. But I remember a few years ago when Open AI's DALLE mini got really popular, how wrapped up in the hype I was, and how fun it was to mess around with the text to image generation. With how insanely quickly generative ai has progressed in the years since, that sense of fun & play feels a bit embarassing to look back on – all the prompting of shitty litle blurry images was feeding into the development of technologies which now have the power to distable our sense of truth, as well as polluting the internet with endless slop. Apart from naiivety, maybe the reason that DALLE mini felt fun & harmless at the time was that it was only capable of generating dreamy, alien images - easily recognisable as ai and also kind of inherently funny because of how surreal they were. There's much more fun in a shoddy, hallucinated image of homer simpson dunking on john bercow than there is in a near-perfect rendering.
<br><br>

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;I think there was maybe more possibility for artistic value as well: the grotesque look of early ai art was at least an identifiable aesthetic, which, in its alien quality, was something different from the human-made art it was trained on and copying. At some point between 2022 and now, that aesthetic, and that artistic barrier has gone - art made with generative ai is capable of near-perfect replication, and it's awful! With no material limitations to push up against, ai art feels like an artform with no real medium, you can just keep prompting until you get what you want, and the ai will obey you all the way. That feels completely at odds with the spirit of human creativity. There needs to be someone, or something, to tell you no! that's not possible! Those restrictions can be surpassed, through invention or new approaches, and that can lead to great art & great new art forms - but if that's a barrier which can be broken, or jumped over, the barrier of ai art feels like one that just keeps on bending: an amorphous, suffocating blobbbbb. 
<br><br>

<hr>
<br>
There's a very famous Brian Eno quote about music & mediums:
<br><br>

<strong>"Whatever you now find weird, ugly, uncomfortable and nasty about a new medium will surely become its signature. CD distortion, the jitteriness of digital video, the crap sound of 8-bit - all of these will be cherished and emulated as soon as they can be avoided. It’s the sound of failure: so much modern art is the sound of things going out of control, of a medium pushing to its limits and breaking apart."</strong>
<br><br>

&nbsp;&nbsp;&nbsp;Does this framework hold in the age of generative ai? I feel like early ai models, like Dalle Mini, sort of work in this way - they were capable of breaking, and producing interesting results, 'weird, ugly, uncomfortable and nasty' things. The surrealism of Dalle mini images was also present in early ai text generation, which was capable of breaking & hallucinating mad phrases. In <a target=_blank href='https://samkriss.substack.com/p/a-users-guide-to-the-zairja-of-the'>a substack article from 2022</a>, Sam Kriss describes trying to productively break early Chat GPT by getting it to generate impossible texts, like 'to explain Armenia-Azerbaijan relations in the form of a recipe for cheese fondue', these prompts were capable of getting properly surreal, inexplicable hallucinations from GPT 2 - but Chat GPT 3 was released, the model had become 'too good' & boooring:
<br><br>

<strong>'GPT-2 was not intended as a machine for being silly, but that’s what it was — precisely because it wasn’t actually very good at generating ordinary text. The vast potential of all possible forms kept on seeping in around the edges, which is how it could generate such strange and beautiful strings of text. But GPT-3 and its applications have managed to close the lid on chaos... It’s worked out the patterns of our language, and now it can reproduce them. It gives you exactly what you ask for: text that’s perfectly lucid and sensical, and dull, dull, dull.'</strong>
